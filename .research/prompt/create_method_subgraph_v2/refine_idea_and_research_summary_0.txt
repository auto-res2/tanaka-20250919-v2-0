# Novelty
None of the papers listed in “Related Works” tackle catastrophic over-specialisation during LLM fine-tuning, nor do they introduce an auxiliary loss that (i) operates in logit space, (ii) is based on a symmetric Jensen–Shannon divergence, (iii) re-weights each token by an on-line Fisher estimate, (iv) couples task and preservation objectives through a schedule tied to validation loss, and (v) uses cheap external ‘anchor’ text instead of multi-task co-training.  The closest ideas in the list are (a) fixed KL regularisation implicitly covered in general scaling–law studies and (b) parameter-wise consolidation such as EWC, but these differ in both mathematical object (parameter‐space vs probability-space) and practical machinery (Hessian masks vs single forward of a frozen teacher).  The zeroth-order and MP3 papers study orthogonal problems (memory-efficient optimisation, self-supervised pretraining) and do not propose any consistency-based preservation loss.  The simultaneous combination of symmetry, Fisher weighting and adaptive annealing therefore constitutes a new variant of regularisation not present in the surveyed literature.

# Significance
Catastrophic forgetting during task-specific fine-tuning is a well-documented barrier to deploying LLMs in real products: loss of factuality, bias audits, or regulatory audits often require a model to retain its general capabilities.  By showing (according to the provided results) a 4–6× reduction in perplexity degradation with <8 % throughput penalty, the method offers an immediately usable tool for industry pipelines that already rely on PEFT/LoRA.  Academically, it extends the family of knowledge-preservation techniques with a theoretically bounded, symmetric divergence that avoids the Hessian computation bottleneck of EWC, making it feasible for 7-13 B-parameter models—a scale at which previous parameter-space methods are rarely applied.  Because the approach is loss-level and architecture-agnostic, it can seed further work on trust-region style objectives for continual and multi-task learning, and could influence benchmarks for robustness and calibration of fine-tuned LLMs.
